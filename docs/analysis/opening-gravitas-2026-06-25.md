## 1. What the coda does that the opening doesn't (the register to match)

The closing paragraphs land because of six moves, all of which the opening mostly lacks:

1. **Long, comma-chained sentences that build to a landing.** *"When the gap between concentrated
   power, concentrated wealth, and the ordinary citizen grows wide enough, the experiment stops
   feeling like it belongs to the people who live under it; and a democracy that no longer answers
   to its people is, sooner or later, a democracy that falls."* Momentum carries the reader.
2. **Aphoristic landings.** "An experiment, not a monument." "One move, two losses." "What no one
   believes in does not get reformed."
3. **Triadic rhythm.** "abandoned, divested from, or torn down." "not in a strict verdict, not in a
   poignant surrender, but in the slow, apathetic withdrawal…"
4. **Historical/literary depth.** Washington's 1789 "experiment entrusted to the hands of the
   American people," Jefferson, the living Constitution.
5. **Escalating stakes, ending on the highest one, earned.** It climbs to democracy falling.
6. **Calm authority with a little swagger.** "And just like this essay, we will bring the receipts
   and the data."

## 2. Where the opening (§0–§2) drops below that register

- **The hook is too casual.** "Let's start with one fact that explains a shocking amount of
  American politics: almost nobody trusts the government anymore." That is a blog/listicle opener.
  The coda's equivalent — "It all comes back to the number we opened with" — is calm and grave. The
  open reaches for a quick hook where it should reach for weight.
- **Staccato fragments deflate momentum** (and cut against your own no-staccato preference):
  "That didn't happen to one party. It happened to every generation, slowly, for sixty years." and
  later "So we checked." The coda never does this; it builds.
- **The data note kills momentum at the worst moment.** §0's THIRD paragraph ("A quick note on the
  data before we begin our argument in earnest…") is a methods digression placed before the argument
  has even started. You ask the reader for gravitas and then hand them a footnote. (Fix below.)
- **The through-line isn't planted with the coda's weight.** The coda frames the trust collapse as
  the American "experiment" failing. The open states the trust numbers as an interesting explanatory
  fact. Foreshadow the experiment/stakes frame in the first lines and the coda pays off like a bell.

## 3. Drafted rewrites of the §0 opening lede (pick one / remix)

All three keep your numbers (3-in-4 → 1-in-5), the every-generation point, and your "we"/essay voice;
all drop the staccato and the casual hook; all are long-sentence and graver.

**A — measured, lands on an aphorism (closest to your current content):**
> One number sits underneath almost everything in this essay. In 1964, about three in four Americans
> told pollsters they trusted the government in Washington to do what is right most of the time; today
> barely one in five say the same. That is not the story of a single party falling out of favor, or a
> single generation souring, it happened to every party, every cohort, every region, slowly and almost
> without anyone deciding it, over the better part of sixty years, until distrust of government became
> the rarest thing in American life: something close to a consensus.

**B — foreshadows the coda's "experiment / withdrawal" frame (best long-range payoff):**
> Begin where the country actually is. In 1964, roughly three in four Americans trusted their
> government to do right by them; today roughly one in five do, and that long slide is the closest
> thing we have to a shared national experience, cutting across party and generation alike. It is less
> a grievance than a verdict, the quiet sound of a people withdrawing its faith from the experiment it
> was handed. Everything this essay argues about who wins elections, and why the safe, electable
> candidate is so often the one who loses, begins in that withdrawal.

**C — keeps your "explains a shocking amount" energy, but elevated:**
> Almost everything strange about recent American politics resolves into a single fact: the country
> has stopped trusting its own government. In 1964, about three in four Americans said they trusted
> Washington to do what is right most of the time; today barely one in five do, a collapse that spared
> no party and no generation and took the better part of sixty years to complete. Hold that number in
> view, because the argument that follows, about the center, the swing voter, and the candidates we are
> told are safe, never strays far from it.

> My lean: **B** for the first lede (it sets up the coda), then keep your existing second §0 lede
> ("It is this collapse in trust, we will argue, that is less a cause than a symptom…"), which already
> sits in the right register.

## 4. The data note (§0 ¶3) — move it

It is the single biggest momentum-killer in the opening. Recommend, in order of preference:
1. **Demote to a footnote** hung on the first sentence that actually leans on presidential-year data
   (early §2 or §3). The three reasons (completeness, presidential races set the terms, executive
   consolidation) are footnote material, not opening material.
2. **Relocate** it to the very start of §3 ("So who actually swings?"), where the data first does
   heavy lifting, as a one-line aside rather than a paragraph.
3. If it must stay in §0, cut it to one sentence and move it to the END of the section, after the
   reader is hooked.

## 5. Small de-staccato fixes (drafts)

- "That didn't happen to one party. It happened to every generation, slowly, for sixty years." →
  *"It did not happen to one party but to every generation at once, slowly, across sixty years."*
- "So we checked." (§1) — keep it ONLY if you want one deliberate short beat for contrast; otherwise
  fold into the prior sentence: *"…and for all the repetition, almost nobody stops to check whether
  it is true, so we did."*
