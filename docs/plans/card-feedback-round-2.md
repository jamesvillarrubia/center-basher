## P2 — argument / framing rewrites

### `pools` — likely cut or rebuild
> "It's sort of true because people conflate the persuadable middle with people who
> didn't vote… independent low-propensity voters are conflated because 'oh, they don't
> like either candidate, therefore they must be in the middle.' But that's not true.
> So it's not really that there's three swingable moderates. I'm just not sure that
> card works. The persuadable middle is not really there, and that's the hard point."

The card may *reinforce* the conflation it means to attack by presenting the two pools
as commensurable. The real point is that the persuadable middle doesn't exist at all.

### `mirage` — antithesis is weak
> "People are saying 'great, it's 5 voters in 100, but those are the 5 we need.' It
> refutes the big-moderate-middle claim but that's not a common line of attack. I'm not
> sure how effective this is. We probably need to rethink that antithesis framing."

### `illusion` — retitle and rewrite sub
- Title → **"Independents and swing voters are not centrists."**
- Sub → independents hold diverse, opposing views that only average to a center when you
  force them onto a single linear left-right spectrum. We don't know which issues they
  actually vote on, and when they are single-issue, it's almost never the centrist one.

### `alienated` — reframe away from "non-voter"
> "Non-voters is not really a term of art. It's not that they're too lazy or don't care.
> Low turnout is deep care plus frustration. They're tired and don't believe their vote
> fixes anything because they did vote and nothing changed."

The argument: both parties sold change, didn't deliver, the rich got richer, and these
voters still care intensely. The lever is delivering material results, not virtue
signaling. Progressive positions are popular with this group.

### `insideoutside` — make "outside" read as the attractive position
> "We need to make it more tied to being outside the system is attractive. People on
> Twitter think Buttigieg being inside is a good thing. No one gives a shit about
> Sanders being to the left of Buttigieg. They care that Sanders is outside and
> Buttigieg is inside."

### `authenticity` — add context and distribution
- Add **Sanders** alongside Trump vs Clinton
- Add a distribution: % of voters and % of *swing* voters by trust level, showing the
  vast majority of swing voters are low-trust and Clinton had no position there

### `badtrade` — diagram doesn't land
> "Title's good, but the diagram isn't. Yet."
- Axis unclear
- Lean-left swing bar should be blue, not red (red reads as "bad thing")
- Bigger reads as better; need to defeat that association
- Reconsider what's included

### `care` — currently tautological
> "'People vote when the result matters to them' is tautological. I'm not sure the
> mobilization-tactics claim holds up — people will say 'our mobilization approach is to
> make people care.'"

Take-home should be **what** they care about: trust, not policy.

### `consistency` — fix the claim, name the causal jump
Claim should be: *swing voters are moderates, therefore be moderate to win swing voters.*
Rebuttal: the vast majority of self-described moderates are actually partisan. Note the
causal inversion — we're told swing voters are moderates, not that moderates are swing
voters.

### `deficit` — add candidates
Add **Biden**. Add **Trump** (both terms may be combined).

### `fundamentals` — retitle
> "Strong economies don't reliably favor incumbents" / "…don't reliably predict
> elections." With the caveat our own paper makes: it did predict, until low trust.
