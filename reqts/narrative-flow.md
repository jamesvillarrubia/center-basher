## /goal
A causally-directional argument that **institutional trust — not left-right — is the axis that
decides the contestable electorate**, using 2016 (Clinton vs Trump, populism) as the worked
example. Show that (a) trust predicts both turnout and vote choice given a candidate's position,
(b) shifting *policy* or *trust* position moves turnout only marginally (policy ≈0), and therefore
(c) you must **pick candidates whose trust-position already matches the base you need** rather than
reposition a high-trust candidate into the well. Reframe to be persuasive to an **anti-Sanders**
audience: center on Clinton/Trump/populism; treat the Sanders case as a counterfactual to *test
with the math*, including the reverse risk.

## Spine (one sentence)
The votes in play sit **low on trust**; establishment candidates **orbit high**; you can't close a
vertical gap with horizontal (policy) moves, so you pick a candidate who **starts in the well**.
