The Center Doesn't Exist

Why "swing voters" aren't moderates — they're the disillusioned

The conventional story

Voters on a line

Political analysts love a simple left-right spectrum. Independents sit in the middle. To win them, you move to the center.

On this view, "Independent" = "moderate." Winning them means triangulating toward the middle on policy.

A different lens

Add trust. The map changes.

What if we plot the same voters on two axes: left-right ideology and trust in institutions — government, Congress, media, courts?

The "center" empties out. Most voters who look moderate on policy actually sit in the low-trust quadrant — not the moderate quadrant.

The punchline

Swing voters aren't in the middle. They're at the bottom.

Voters who switched parties between elections — the "swing" — cluster in the low-trust half of the map, not at the policy center.

Political gravity in 2D space runs toward anti-institution candidates, not toward moderate-policy candidates. That's why the same pool of voters considered Bernie Sanders and Donald Trump — both ran against "the system."

So what?

Moving to the "center" is the wrong map

If the dominant axis for swing voters is trust vs. distrust — not left vs. right — then a party that moderates its policy positions to appeal to the center is solving the wrong problem.

  • Bernie → Trump, not Bernie → Hillary. On a 1D map, Hillary is between Bernie and Trump. In 2D trust-space, Trump and Bernie are in the same low-trust hemisphere. Hillary is not.
  • "Drain the swamp" is a trust message, not a policy message. It works because the target audience is the low-trust quadrant.
  • Winning these voters requires institutional credibility, not policy centrism. Or a credible anti-institution posture. There is no third option.

Methodology

Data: American National Election Studies (ANES) 2016 Time Series. Ideology axis: 7-point liberal-conservative self-placement (V161126), rescaled −1 to +1. Trust index: average of four institutional trust items (federal government, Congress, Supreme Court, news media), recoded so higher = more trust, normalized 0–1.
