The claim “Sanders and Warren were unelectable. Their health plans were fringe.”
The “unelectable” ones were standing where the voters are.
Where voters placed themselves in 2024 on the survey’s own government-vs-private insurance scale, against where the 2020 primary field stood. The most popular single position in the country is still the one that got called fringe, and the private-insurance end has shrunk since. The candidates who ran on it were the ones told they couldn’t win.
Voter curve: ANES 2024 Time Series, POST weight (V240107b), n=3,432 voters, on the survey’s 7-point government-vs-private medical insurance item (V241245); endpoint wording verbatim. The item is worded identically in 2016, 2020 and 2024, and the government-insurance end has been the most-chosen position in all three (22.2% in 2020, 21.6% in 2024). Candidate positions are their stated 2020 primary platforms (KFF; Time; Washington Post) mapped onto that scale, so read them as ordering, not as measurements.
