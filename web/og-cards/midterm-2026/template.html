2026 midterms · the data

Who made the 2026 majorities?

Third Way says moderates did. We scored 44 Democratic nominees on what they said they would do, and checked their margins against seven baselines.

The 44 nominees

We scored every Democratic nominee in the 14 competitive Senate races and in the 30 House races where Democrats fought a contested primary. These are the races where Third Way, the party establishment and outside money picked sides. Two researchers, working separately and without seeing any election results, read each nominee's campaign site, debates, interviews, questionnaires and votes. They scored 13 positions, from Medicare for All to ending the filibuster: +1 for support, 0 for silence, −1 for opposition. Every non-zero score cites the nominee's own words.

What we found

  • 412 of 417 House Democrats ran ahead of Harris. One shared shift accounts for 80% of their gains.
  • All 44 nominees we scored took positions to the left of Third Way's, including all 19 that Third Way calls moderate.
  • Third Way endorsed Talarico and disagrees with him on 9 of 13 positions. He ran 25.8 points ahead of Harris, more than any other Senate nominee.
  • Progressive nominees ran as far ahead as everyone else. The most progressive third beat Harris by 14.2 points. The least progressive third beat her by 15.9, a gap well inside the noise.
  • Third Way's Senate edge falls from 12 points to between 6 and 10 against baselines that use no presidential race. In the House, no baseline shows its moderates ahead by more than chance.
  • No candidate trait reliably predicts who ran furthest ahead. We tested 15, including name recognition, money and time on the trail. Incumbency comes closest.

What the data can't show

  • Progressive positions did not raise margins either. The data support "progressive nominees won and paid no measurable penalty." They do not support "progressive positions caused the gains."
  • Establishment backing helped in the House. Establishment-backed House nominees beat the history-based baseline by 6.0 points (95% range +1.2 to +10.8), and 3 of 4 baselines without Harris agree. Backing is not moderation: every one of these nominees still scored left of Third Way.
  • The correction cannot invent a position. It fills each gap from pre-primary facts and carries the uncertainty through. A headless-browser pass over the campaign sites of the 23 least-documented nominees is collecting the pages that the first pass could not read.
  • The sourced-items-only score shows a small negative slope. Three recorded votes drive it: Ossoff and Gonzalez for the GENIUS crypto bill, McAdams against a $15 minimum wage. All three ran 20 to 26 points ahead of Harris.
  • Days on the trail uses announcement dates. FEC filing dates were not available.

How we know

Races. 14 competitive Senate races and 30 House races with contested Democratic primaries; 417 Democrat-vs-Republican House races for the wave. Results from CNN race pages, Nov 5 2026.

Progressive index. 13 positions (Medicare for All, a billionaire tax, no corporate PAC money, no AIPAC money or conditions on Israel aid, Green New Deal, a $17 minimum wage, ending the filibuster, Supreme Court expansion, student debt relief or free college, breaking up monopolies, no crypto money, universal childcare or pre-K, opposing Schumer or Jeffries as leader). Two coders scored each nominee blind to results (Cohen's κ 0.45, no opposite-sign disagreements). Coverage correction: two-way fixed effects for which items were findable, then empirical-Bayes shrinkage toward a prediction from progressive-group endorsement, Third Way's label, establishment backing, primary money against the nominee, incumbency and chamber.

Baselines. Harris 2024; Biden 2020; the prior race for the seat; the 2022 midterm; an expected margin fitted from Biden 2020 and the prior race only (R² 0.95, leave-one-state-out); the 2026 statewide House vote and the 2026 governor race on the same ballot (Senate only).
