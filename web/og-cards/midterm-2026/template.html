A wave lifted every kind of Democrat. Third Way's "moderates" ran on progressive platforms.

Third Way and Neera Tanden credit moderates with the 2026 Democratic majorities. 412 of 417 Democratic House nominees ran ahead of Harris, whatever their label. Scored on what they said they would do, all 44 nominees in the coded sample sit to the left of Third Way's own platform. None of the 14 candidate factors tested predicts who ran furthest ahead.

The progressive index does not predict overperformance in either direction. With chamber and state-clustered intervals, the slope is −0.6 points per +0.1 (95% CI −1.9 to +0.8). The all-44 fit tilts toward the less progressive end because the index tracks documentation (r 0.92 with positions sourced): 17 nominees have zero or one sourced position and score near zero by default. Supported claim: "Progressive nominees won and paid no measurable penalty." Unsupported: "Progressive positions caused the overperformance."

Sources and method

Results: CNN race pages, 2026-11-05. Baselines: Harris 2024, Biden 2020, the prior race for each seat, the 2022 midterm, the 2026 statewide House vote. Factors: Third Way's labels, pre-primary establishment backing, the 13-item progressive index, incumbency, prior office, fame, Wikipedia pageviews, days on the trail, money per vote, outside spending, AIPAC/crypto/AI primary money, terrain and chamber. Findings, footnotes and correction blocks: reqts/midterm-2026-moderate-overperformance.md on branch analysis/2026-moderate-overperformance, PR #13.

Known gaps: JavaScript-only campaign sites were not read, so 17 nominees have zero or one sourced position. Days on the trail uses announcement dates; FEC launch dates are missing for 17 nominees.
