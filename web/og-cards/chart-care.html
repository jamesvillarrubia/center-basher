The claim “Fix turnout with better outreach: more doors, more ads, more enthusiasm.”
People show up when the stakes are real, not when they like you.
2016 turnout, by whether the voter felt the result mattered. That gap is the single biggest turnout lever in the data, and it survives even among low-engagement voters (52% vs 26%). Warmth toward your own candidate, by contrast, doesn’t move turnout at all. A campaign that runs as the safe, inoffensive choice is lowering the one thing turnout depends on.
Source: ANES 2016, weighted (V160101). Turnout proxy = cast a presidential vote (V162034a non-missing), by “care a good deal who wins” (V161005): 70.5% (n=3,117) vs 35.9% (n=1,137), a 34.6-point gap, the largest single bivariate turnout predictor in the file. The gap holds among low-engagement voters (52% vs 26%). In the weighted turnout model, warmth-to-favorite is slightly negative (−0.10) once stakes are accounted for.
