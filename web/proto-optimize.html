Assumptions baked into this model — the parts to argue with:
  1. Voters choose by spatial proximity to candidate positions (anisotropic distance). The trust thesis says low-trust voters don't respond to policy proximity at all — this model can't represent that.
  2. Only Clinton moves; Trump is pinned at his voter centroid.
  3. "Moveable" = undecided-but-likely + decided-but-low-turnout (n≈918). Locked-in voters excluded.
  4. Each voter picks Clinton / Trump / abstain via softmax; abstain utility is calibrated so the baseline matches the moveable group's real split (~31/36/33).
  5. ρ = how much trust distance counts vs ideology distance. ρ=1 euclidean; field-fit ≈0.53 (ideology counts more); ρ>1 = trust counts more.
  6. "Moving down on trust" treats trust as a coordinate Clinton can relocate to — in reality it's perceived legitimacy she couldn't freely dial.
