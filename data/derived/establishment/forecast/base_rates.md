# Leadership election base rates (historical, no forecast)

Compiled 2026-11-05 from sources fetched this session. Each number carries a source link. "Uncertain" marks a count that sources disagree on or that a summarizer returned without a second source. "Not found" marks a search that returned nothing usable. This file makes no prediction about January 2027.

Method notes:

- Web pages came through a fetch tool that summarizes the page. Table values from the CRS report and the House Clerk were returned as reproduced rows. They were not independently re-read from the raw page.
- Majority-party seats are election-day counts from the House History table. The count at the day of the vote differs when members have died, resigned, or been absent.
- The column "Seat shortfall" is majority seats minus the nominee's ballot-1 votes. It includes absences and vacancies, so it overstates defections, most of all before 1995. Named defection counts appear in the notes where a source gives one.

Sources used throughout:

- CRS RL30857, Table 1 (votes by ballot): https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-04-13_RL30857_f8b446324f7e7e361e427f7eafcf31f772fcb5d3.html
- House History party divisions: https://history.house.gov/Institution/Party-Divisions/Party-Divisions/

### Summary statistics

Counts below come from the two tables above.

- Total elections: 58 (52 regular, 6 special). Two special elections (1936, 1940) were voice votes. Of the 56 roll-call elections, 3 needed more than one ballot (1923, Jan 2023, Oct 2023). Decided on ballot 1: 53 of 56 (94.6%). CRS names the same three: https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-04-13_RL30857_f8b446324f7e7e361e427f7eafcf31f772fcb5d3.html
- Seat shortfall distribution, regular elections where control did not change, excluding 1923 (n = 41): median 6, 20 of 41 at 5 or fewer, 13 of 41 at 10 or more. Shortfall includes absences, so these are upper bounds on defections.
- Named defections, modern era (from the notes above): 2013 about 9 to 12 (uncertain), Jan 2015 24, Oct 2015 10, 2019 15, 2021 5, Jan 2023 19 on ballot 1, Oct 2023 20 on Jordan ballot 1, 2025 3 announced then 1 recorded.
- Elections held right after a party won the majority (9 with a usable seat count: 1947, 1949, 1953, 1955, 1995, 2007, 2011, 2019, 2023-Jan; 1931 excluded as uncertain): shortfalls 2, 8, 1, 4, 2, 0, 1, 15, 19. Median 2. Two of nine had 10 or more, both since 2019. Of the 9, the only multi-ballot election was Jan 2023 (1 of 9). The 1923 election was not a control change.
- Named-defection pattern for the two majorities that were newly won with a narrow margin and had public holdouts: 2019 (D 235, 15 defections, 1 ballot) and Jan 2023 (R 222, 19 defections, 15 ballots).

## 5. Base rates

Every rate below has a numerator, a denominator, and the rows that make it up. Event sets are small. The definitions are mine, set before counting, and the lists are the verified cases from sections 1 to 4. A case without a verified source is excluded or flagged.

### 5a. P(sitting leader keeps the leader post | an organized challenge exists)

Definition A, a declared opposing candidate with a recorded vote against a sitting party leader: 4 of 4.

- Pelosi 2010 (150-43), Pelosi 2016 (134-63), McCarthy 2022 (188-31), McConnell 2022 (37-10-1). Sources in sections 2 and 3.

Definition B, any organized effort to remove or replace a sitting leader, including efforts with no ballot: 6 of 9 kept the post at the end of that episode.

- Kept: Pelosi 2010, Pelosi 2016, Pelosi 2018, McCarthy 2022, McConnell 2022, Gingrich 1997 (kept the post through the 1998 elections).
- Did not keep: Boehner 2015 (resigned), Lott 2002 (resigned), McCarthy Oct 2023 (removed 216-210).
- McCarthy Jan 2023 is excluded from the count because it was a Speaker election, shown in 5b. Ranges: 4 of 4 (definition A) to 6 of 9 (definition B).

### 5b. P(Speaker nominee wins on ballot 1 | public holdouts before the vote)

Verified pre-vote holdouts: 4 of 5 won on ballot 1.

- Won ballot 1: Boehner Jan 2015 (Weber, Amash named), Pelosi 2019 (16 signers), Pelosi 2021 (Slotkin said before the vote she would vote present), Johnson 2025 (Massie said he would vote no; Norman and Self switched during the vote).
- Did not: McCarthy Jan 2023 (at least five Republicans refused before the vote, per Politico via Wikipedia; won on ballot 15).
- Flagged cases: Jordan Oct 2023 failed ballot 1 (public pre-vote refusals were mostly private counts; Jordan allies estimated 8 to 10 holdouts; uncertain), which makes the rate 4 of 6 if included as a miss. Ryan Oct 2015 won ballot 1 (nine voted Webster; named pre-vote public refusals not found), which makes it 5 of 7 if both flagged cases are included.
- Uninformative comparison: all 56 roll-call elections, 53 of 56 decided on ballot 1 (94.6%).
- Boehner 2013 had defections of about 9 to 12 and won ballot 1; whether holdouts were public beforehand was not found, so it is excluded.

### 5c. P(revolt ends in concessions rather than replacement)

Revolts counted (12): Pelosi 2010, Pelosi 2016, Pelosi 2018, McCarthy Jan 2023, McConnell 2022, Gingrich 1997, Johnson 2025, Boehner 2015, Lott 2002, McCarthy Oct 2023, Scalise Oct 2023, Jordan Oct 2023.

- Leader stayed with a verified concession: 2 of 12 (Pelosi 2018, McCarthy Jan 2023).
- Leader or nominee replaced: 5 of 12 (Boehner 2015, Lott 2002, McCarthy Oct 2023, Scalise Oct 2023, Jordan Oct 2023).
- Leader stayed with no verified concession: 5 of 12 (Pelosi 2010, Pelosi 2016, McConnell 2022, Gingrich 1997, Johnson 2025).
- Concessions versus replacement, among revolts that ended in one or the other: 2 of 7. Among revolts where the leader was not replaced: 2 of 7 involved a concession.
- Weakness: "no concession found" is a search result, not proof of none. Johnson 2025 involved a Trump call and a separate Nov 2024 rules deal that may count as a concession; excluded here because the page links no concession to the 2025 floor switch.
- Both concession cases later failed to hold: Pelosi's rule was not adopted (as of Jul 2019) though she kept the pledge in practice; McCarthy was removed by the rule he conceded.

### 5d. Related rates already in this file

- Speakers who won a newly gained majority on ballot 1: 8 of 9 (all except McCarthy Jan 2023); the sole multi-ballot case came with a narrow margin.
- Senate: sitting party leader lost a caucus vote: 0 found (denominator: 1 contested vote with a recorded count, McConnell 2022). Sitting leader forced to resign under pressure: 1 found (Lott 2002) among 28 leaders since 1923 (16 Republican and 11 Democratic leaders listed in CRS, plus Thune).
