# Leadership playbook: how congressional party leaders keep power while appeasing dissident factions

Compiled 2026-11-05 from web sources read this session. This file documents past cases only. It makes no forecast.

## 1. Term-limit promises for leaders

### Case 1.1: Pelosi, December 2018

- **Who, when:** Nancy Pelosi, December 12, 2018.
- **What was given, to whom:** A deal to let the caucus vote on a limit of three terms in each of the top three leadership posts (Speaker, Majority Leader, Whip), with a fourth term allowed on a two-thirds caucus vote. Terms served from 2007 to 2011 would count. It went to the Democrats who had vowed to block her Speaker bid, led in the talks by Ed Perlmutter, with Seth Moulton and Tim Ryan on the joint statement. Pelosi's statement said she was "comfortable with the proposal and it is my intention to abide by it, whether it passes or not."
- **Adopted as a binding caucus rule?** No. The proposal was left out of the January 3 rules package and slated for a vote by February 15, 2019. A shutdown led to postponement. By July 26, 2019, Perlmutter said "We're just letting it sit right now" and said he was personally inclined to drop it. Hoyer and Clyburn opposed term limits. Roll Call (July 26, 2019) found no caucus vote had been held.
- **Honored?** Personally, yes. On November 18, 2020 Pelosi said "What I said then is whether it passes or not, I will abide by those limits that are there." On November 17, 2022 she said "I will not seek reelection to Democratic leadership in the next Congress." Roll Call describes this as the end of the self-imposed two-more-terms limit.
- **Sources:**
  - [Hill, Dec 12, 2018](https://digital-release.thehill.com/?p=421120) [F]
  - [WGBH, Dec 12, 2018](https://www.wgbh.org/news/politics/2018-12-12/nancy-pelosi-cuts-deal-with-democratic-rebels-to-ensure-return-to-speakership) [F]
  - [Roll Call, Jul 26, 2019](https://www.rollcall.com/2019/07/26/they-wanted-term-limits-for-leadership-pelosi-agreed-now-what/) [F]
  - [Roll Call, Nov 18, 2020](https://rollcall.com/2020/11/18/pelosi-affirms-promise-that-next-term-as-speaker-will-be-her-last/) [F]
  - [Roll Call, Nov 17, 2022](https://rollcall.com/2022/11/17/pelosi-opts-to-step-down-as-democratic-leader/) [F]

### Case 1.2: House Republican Speaker limit, adopted 1995, repealed 2003

- **Who, when:** House Republican Conference. The 1994-95 reform package set an eight-year limit on the Speaker and six-year limits on committee chairs. The conference voted to lift the Speaker limit at its first meeting of the 108th Congress (January 2003), for Speaker Dennis Hastert.
- **What was given, to whom:** This limit was a binding conference rule from adoption. The repeal went the other direction: the conference removed a constraint on the leader, stated as a show of support for Hastert. Chair limits stayed.
- **Source date caveat:** The Fox News page carries a 2015 date stamp. The text describes the 108th Congress and Hastert, so the stamp is wrong. The month of the repeal is not in the page.
- **Source:** [Fox News, "GOP seeks to lift term limits on House speaker"](https://www.foxnews.com/story/gop-seeks-to-lift-term-limits-on-house-speaker.print) [F]. The American Spectator confirms the 2003 repeal [S] (page returned HTTP 403).

### Case 1.3: Foster committee-leader retention vote, December 13, 2022 (a rejected case)

- **Who, when:** Rep. Bill Foster (D-Ill.) proposed a six-year limit on service as chair or ranking member, with a caucus vote on waivers. The Democratic Caucus voted it down 138-63 on December 13, 2022. Roll Call does not report positions of Jeffries or Pelosi.
- **Source:** [Roll Call, Dec 13, 2022](https://www.rollcall.com/2022/12/13/house-democrats-reject-committee-term-limit-proposal/) [F]

### Outcome (tactic 1)

The concession moved no agenda control, gavels, money or votes. Pelosi's pledge bound only Pelosi, was never put to the caucus, and she kept the Speakership for the 116th and 117th Congresses as announced. The Republican limit (case 1.2) was binding for about eight years until the conference repealed it. Evidence: Roll Call July 26, 2019 (no vote, rebel lead negotiator dropped it); Roll Call Nov 17, 2022 (step-down on the announced timeline).

## 2. New or expanded leadership posts for dissidents or newcomers

### Case 2.1: Sanders as Senate Democratic outreach chair, November 2016

- **Who, when:** Senate Democratic leadership meeting, November 16, 2016. Sanders became Chair of Outreach (a new post) and kept the Budget Committee ranking seat. Warren became Vice Chair of the Conference. Time says the Sanders post "recognizes his increased visibility after a populist election season."
- **Powers:** The Time article does not describe the staff, budget or duties of either post. Not found elsewhere this session.
- **Later:** Sanders is listed as outreach chair on the 119th Congress team (late 2024) [S].
- **Sources:** [Time, Nov 16, 2016](https://time.com/4573592/bernie-sanders-elizabeth-warren-democrats) [F]; [Senate Democrats, 119th leadership release](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/majority-leader-schumer-announces-senate-democratic-leadership-team-for-the-119th-congress) [S].

### Case 2.2: Booker's new Strategic Communications chair, December 2024

- **Who, when:** After the 2024 election, Schumer's team created a Strategic Communications Committee chair for Cory Booker, ranked fourth. One report says it resolved a clash between Klobuchar and Booker over the No. 3 slot. Klobuchar took the Steering and Policy chair.
- **Sources:** [Hill, Dec 2024](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/5019416-senate-democrats-re-elect-schumer/amp) [S]; [Booker release](https://www.booker.senate.gov/news/press/booker-elected-to-senate-democratic-leadership-team-for-the-119th-congress) [S].
- **Note:** Booker is a rival for rank, not a progressive insurgent. The case is included because it shows the same mechanism (a new post to settle a contest).

### Case 2.3: Pelosi creates a DPCC chair for Cicilline, November 2018

- **Who, when:** Pelosi proposed a new DPCC Chair post in a November 2018 letter. Rep. David Cicilline dropped out of the assistant-leader race against Ben Ray Lujan and was elected to the new post on November 29, 2018, unopposed. The post outranks the three DPCC co-chairs (Bustos, Jeffries, Cartwright).
- **Sources:** [Roll Call, Nov 21, 2018](https://www.rollcall.com/2018/11/21/cicilline-drops-out-of-assistant-democratic-leader-race/) [S]; [Roll Call, Nov 29, 2018](https://www.rollcall.com/2018/11/29/house-democrats-elect-4-members-to-run-messaging-arm/) [S].

### Case 2.4: Pelosi gives Barbara Lee a Steering and Policy co-chair, December 2018

- **Who, when:** Lee lost the caucus chair race to Jeffries. Pelosi then helped her win a Steering and Policy co-chair seat, shared with Eric Swalwell and Rosa DeLauro (December 4, 2018). Pelosi said the "historic size, energy and diversity" of the majority needed more representation. Co-chair count was expanded to three.
- **Source:** [California Globe, Dec 4, 2018](https://californiaglobe.com/?p=7706) [F]; [Pelosi release](https://pelosi.house.gov/news/press-releases/pelosi-announces-steering-and-policy-committee-co-chairs-members-0) [S].

### Freshman leadership representatives

Not found for 2018 or later this session. One search result mentioned Jamie Raskin as freshman representative in the 115th Congress [S]. No source on how the post affects Steering votes.

### Outcome (tactic 2)

Symbolic or unproven. No retrieved source documents a change in agenda control, gavels, money or floor votes tied to any of the four posts. The Time article says nothing about powers. The Cicilline post is described as a landing spot that resolved a contested race. The leader's benefit is documented (a contest ended); the transfer of power to the dissident side is not documented.

## 3. Steering and committee-assignment rewards and punishments

Committees named in the request: Appropriations and Ways and Means had no case found this session. Energy and Commerce, Financial Services, Rules and Budget had cases.

### Case 3.1: Boehner removes four Republicans from committees, December 4, 2012 (punishment)

- **Who, when:** The House Republican Steering Committee, under Speaker Boehner, removed Tim Huelskamp and Justin Amash from Budget, Walter Jones and David Schweikert from Financial Services, and (per other reporting) Huelskamp from Agriculture. Reason reported: they "bucked party leaders on key votes." Schweikert's office said he was told he voted "against the team." Boehner wrote that "there is no scorecard or any other single criteria."
- **Sources:** [Washington Examiner, Dec 4, 2012](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=389213) [F]; [MPR/AP, Dec 5, 2012](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2012/12/05/gop-leaders-remove-4-from-plum-house-committees) [S].
- **Correction:** An earlier version of the task framing named Stutzman. No source found this session reports his removal.

### Case 3.2: McCarthy seats Freedom Caucus members on Rules, January 2023 (reward)

- **Who, when:** Chip Roy, Ralph Norman and Thomas Massie "won Rules seats" in early 2023 as part of McCarthy's Speaker bid. Roy said "We're now going to have conservative representation on that committee."
- **Result:** On May 30, 2023 the Rules Committee advanced the debt-limit rule 7-6. Roy and Norman voted no; Massie voted yes after winning a 1 percent sequester provision.
- **Sources:** [Roll Call, May 30, 2023](https://rollcall.com/2023/05/30/debt-limit-deal-clears-key-hurdle-more-land-mines-await/) [F]; [CT Public/NPR, Jan 9, 2023](https://www.ctpublic.org/2023-01-09/house-rules-changes-breeze-through-the-chamber-following-a-bitter-speaker-fight) [F].

### Case 3.3: McCarthy removes Schiff, Swalwell and Omar, 2023 (punishment of the minority party)

- **Who, when:** McCarthy rejected Schiff and Swalwell for the Intelligence Committee (January 24-25, 2023, no floor vote needed). The House voted to remove Ilhan Omar from Foreign Affairs on February 2, 2023.
- **Source:** [Axios, Jan 25, 2023](https://www.axios.com/2023/01/25/mccarthy-removes-schiff-swalwell-intelligence-committee) [S]; [Axios, Feb 2, 2023](https://www.axios.com/2023/02/02/house-votes-remove-ilhan-omar-foreign-affairs-committee) [S].
- **Note:** These targeted opposition-party members, not a faction inside the leader's own party. They show the tool, and are not a dissident-appeasement case.

### Case 3.4: Democrats deny, then grant, Ocasio-Cortez an Energy and Commerce seat

- **2020:** On December 17, 2020 the Steering and Policy Committee voted 46-13 by secret ballot to give a seat to Kathleen Rice over Ocasio-Cortez. The four others chosen were New Dem members. Reported factors: her public criticism of Pelosi and her Medicare for All and Green New Deal positions. Rep. Henry Cuellar cited her support for a primary challenger (Jessica Cisneros). Pelosi chairs Steering and Policy and had campaigned for Cuellar before his primary. The article does not say Pelosi opposed her directly.
- **2025:** On January 8, 2025 E&E News reported the panel selected her and five other Democrats for Energy and Commerce after retirements. She said she had sought the seat for several years. The article does not mention Jeffries. Her own statement thanked Jeffries and the committee (per search summary [S]).
- **Sources:** [Common Dreams, Dec 18, 2020](https://commondreams.org/news/2020/12/18/secret-ballot-vote-house-democrats-overwhelmingly-deny-aoc-powerful-committee-seat) [F]; [E&E News, Jan 8, 2025](https://www.eenews.net/articles/how-ocasio-cortez-landed-a-seat-on-energy-and-commerce/) [F].

### Outcome (tactic 3)

Real power, in both directions. Committee seats gate legislation. Evidence: Rules seats gave Roy, Norman and Massie a seven-vote threshold story and a chance to oppose a rule in committee (Roll Call, May 30, 2023); the 2012 removals took Budget and Financial Services seats from four Republicans (Washington Examiner); the 2020 Steering vote kept the most visible progressive off the committee with jurisdiction over her two signature issues for four years (Common Dreams). Lee's Steering co-chair (case 2.4) is a smaller version of the reward. A reward moves power only when the seat is on a committee that writes the dissident's issue. Seat counts for Appropriations and Ways and Means: not found.

## 4. Rules-package concessions

### Case 4.1: McCarthy's January 2023 deal

- **Who, when:** Kevin McCarthy, January 3-9, 2023, after 15 ballots.
- **What was given, to whom:** To the holdouts: a one-member motion to vacate (restoring the pre-2019 rule after a five-member offer was rejected), Rules Committee seats (case 3.2), 72 hours' notice before votes, a Select Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government, a plan to move 12 individual appropriations bills, and a spending goal of fiscal 2022 domestic levels (French Hill: "aspirational"). The package passed with Tony Gonzales the only Republican no vote.
- **Result:** On October 3, 2023 the House voted 216-210 to remove McCarthy on a motion filed by Matt Gaetz on October 2.
- **Sources:** [CT Public/NPR, Jan 9, 2023](https://www.ctpublic.org/2023-01-09/house-rules-changes-breeze-through-the-chamber-following-a-bitter-speaker-fight) [F]; [Wikipedia, Ouster of Kevin McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouster_of_Kevin_McCarthy) [F].

### Case 4.2: Democratic 2019 package (PAYGO, motion-to-vacate change, Break the Gridlock)

- **Who, when:** House passed Title I 234-197 on January 3, 2019. Khanna, Ocasio-Cortez and Gabbard voted no over pay-as-you-go. Omar said Rules Chair McGovern and leadership assured progressives PAYGO "will not be used to block key progressive priorities this Congress." The Progressive Caucus co-chairs (Pocan, Jayapal) promised legislation to repeal the PAYGO statute. Roll Call notes the House rule can be waived by a vote and often is.
- **Motion to vacate:** Democrats made the motion privileged only when offered at a party conference's direction. That change protected the leader. It did not concede power to dissidents.
- **Source:** [Roll Call, Jan 3, 2019](https://www.rollcall.com/2019/01/03/house-adopts-rules-package-with-few-democratic-defections-over-paygo-provision/) [F]; motion-to-vacate details from the search summary [S].

### Case 4.3: Problem Solvers deal with Pelosi, November 28, 2018

- **Who, when:** Pelosi, Rep. McGovern and nine Problem Solvers Caucus Democrats (co-chair Josh Gottheimer), November 28, 2018. Eight holdouts committed to backing her.
- **What was given:** (1) timely floor consideration of bills with at least 290 co-sponsors (already agreed earlier); (2) a Rules Committee protocol that "specifically adds a preference" for amendments with 20 co-sponsors per party, softened from a guaranteed vote; (3) committee markups only for bills requested by a majority of a committee's members, narrowed from the original ask. Rep. Kurt Schrader, outside the deal, said "it's not nearly what we put in for our request."
- **Source:** [Roll Call, Nov 28, 2018](https://rollcall.com/2018/11/28/problem-solvers-to-back-pelosi-for-speaker-after-reaching-agreement-on-rules-changes/) [F].

### Outcome (tactic 4)

Mixed. The 2023 McCarthy package transferred real procedural power: the one-member motion to vacate removed him nine months later, and the Rules seats gave three members committee votes. The 2019 Democratic package gave progressives a verbal assurance on PAYGO (unverified by Roll Call) and the Problem Solvers a softened preference in place of a guarantee (Roll Call, Nov 28, 2018). The 2019 motion-to-vacate change ran in the leader's favor.

## 5. Messaging bills and show votes

### Case 5.1: Medicare for All Rules Committee hearing, April 30, 2019

- **Who, when:** Rules Chair McGovern, Jayapal and Dingell announced on April 23, 2019 a hearing on H.R. 1384, held April 30. The release calls it the first congressional hearing on Medicare for All. The bill had more than 100 cosponsors. Ways and Means Chair Neal planned his own hearing, per search summary [S].
- **Floor vote:** The release says nothing about a floor vote. This session found no source reporting a floor vote in the 116th Congress. Not found.
- **Source:** [Rules Committee release, Apr 23, 2019](https://rules.house.gov/media/press-releases/chairman-mcgovern-congresswoman-jayapal-and-congresswoman-dingell-announce) [F].

### Case 5.2: Select Committee on the Climate Crisis, announced December 2018

- **Who, when:** Pelosi named Kathy Castor chair. Route Fifty (published January 2019) reports the committee likely lacks subpoena power and cannot draft legislation. Sunrise's Stephen O'Hanlon called it "a big disappointment."
- **Source:** [Route Fifty, Jan 2019](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2019/01/democrats-house-climate-crisis-committee/153866/) [F].

### Case 5.3: Build Back Better procedural vote, November 5, 2021

- **Who, when:** Pelosi paired the bipartisan infrastructure vote with a procedural vote on Build Back Better rather than a final vote, and the White House said House passage would come the week of November 15. Progressives led by Jayapal voted to advance infrastructure. Manchin announced in December 2021 he could not support Build Back Better in its present form, per one search-summary source.
- **Sources:** [Roll Call, Nov 5, 2021](https://rollcall.com/2021/11/05/house-sends-infrastructure-bill-to-biden-without-budget-vote/) [S]; [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/magazine-features/1436557/charge-of-the-left-brigade/) [S]. House final passage tally: not found this session.

### Case 5.4: Senate Green New Deal vote, March 26, 2019 (leader-forced, for contrast)

- **Who, when:** Senate Republicans under McConnell forced a 57-0 procedural vote. Forty-three Democrats voted present; Markey, the co-sponsor, did too. Schumer said "I'm for it, but I have no problem voting 'present.'" Democrats called it a sham vote. This was the majority leader using a show vote against the minority's left flank.
- **Source:** [UPI, Mar 26, 2019](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/US/2019/03/26/Senate-stymies-Green-New-Deal-as-Democrats-protest-sham-vote/4871553612843/?sl=10) [F].

### McCarthy's term-limits vote promise, January 2023

A promised floor vote on congressional term limits appeared in search summaries as part of the McCarthy deal, one commentator calling it "just symbolism." The fetched CT Public/NPR article does not mention it. Treat as unverified. Source: [Rolling Stone, Jan 2023](https://www.rollingstone.com/politics/politics-news/kevin-mccarthy-concessions-speakership-unclear-1234656241/) [S].

### Outcome (tactic 5)

Symbolic, with one partial. Hearings and a select committee without subpoena or drafting power produced no law. The Climate Crisis committee has documented limits (Route Fifty). The Medicare for All hearing gave the bill committee exposure but no floor vote was found. Build Back Better received a procedural vote and no enactment (Manchin, December 2021, [S]). The leader controls the schedule and gives nothing the Senate or White House can veto.

## 6. Primary threats and money

### Case 6.1: DCCC vendor blacklist, March 2019 to March 2021

- **Who, when:** DCCC chair Cheri Bustos, announced late March 2019. The policy barred the DCCC from hiring or recommending vendors that worked for primary challengers to House Democratic incumbents. The Progressive Caucus leadership (Khanna, Jayapal, Pocan) met Bustos and Khanna said they "strongly oppose" it (The Intercept, March 27, 2019). Justice Democrats launched DCCCBlacklist.com in early April 2019. Ocasio-Cortez called it "extremely divisive & harmful to the party."
- **End:** New chair Sean Patrick Maloney removed the vendor attestation in March 2021: "I don't think the blanket ban ever made sense." He added that vendors "should [not] be looking for work around here if they want to go after one of our members." Justice Democrats warned that a formal ban could be replaced by an informal one.
- **Sources:** [The Intercept, Mar 27, 2019](https://theintercept.com/2019/03/27/progressive-caucus-slams-dccc-head-cheri-bustos-for-her-assault-on-primary-challengers) [F]; [Hill, Mar 2021](https://digital-release.thehill.com/homenews/campaign/542286-house-democrats-campaign-arm-lifts-ban-on-consultants-who-work-for-primary) [S]; [Common Dreams, Mar 9, 2021](https://commondreams.org/news/2021/03/09/enormous-win-progressives-hail-official-end-dccc-consultant-blacklist) [S].

### Case 6.2: AIPAC / United Democracy Project against Bowman and Bush, 2024

- **Bowman:** AIPAC, UDP and Democratic Majority for Israel "reportedly spent $14.5 million" against Jamaal Bowman, reported by the Times of Israel on June 22, 2024 citing the New York Times, for challenger George Latimer. Bowman lost his June 25, 2024 primary.
- **Bush:** KQED reports UDP spent "over $5 million" against Cori Bush; WLRN and WORLD put the Bush figure above $8 million [S]. Bush lost in August 2024.
- **Total:** KQED reports UDP spent about $37 million in 2024, up from $26 million in 2022.
- **Leader role:** The Washington Examiner reports Jeffries "avoids taking sides" (2024) [S]. No source retrieved shows Jeffries or Schumer directing UDP money. A Breaking Points video alleging routing of AIPAC cash through a Jeffries fund was seen only as a headline and is unverified.
- **Sources:** [Times of Israel, Jun 22, 2024](https://www.timesofisrael.com/aipac-leads-unprecedented-14-5-million-campaign-against-bowman-in-ny-primary/) [F]; [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12016915/aipac-spent-big-in-the-2024-election-how-did-the-money-show-up-in-californias-congressional-races) [F]; [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=3062316) [S].

### Case 6.3: Leadership-adjacent primary support for incumbents

- **Cuellar, March 2020:** Per a Washington Post item reprinted by EMILY's List [S], Pelosi and Hoyer made a last-minute fundraising visit for Rep. Henry Cuellar before his primary. The DCCC kept supporting Rep. Dan Lipinski, who lost to Marie Newman 47.1% to 44.8% on March 17, 2020 [S]. No source found Pelosi personally backing Lipinski.
- **Sources:** [NPR, Mar 18, 2020](https://www.npr.org/2020/03/18/817667843/democratic-house-incumbent-ousted-by-progressive-challenger-in-illinois-primary) [S]; [Common Dreams, Dec 18, 2020](https://commondreams.org/news/2020/12/18/secret-ballot-vote-house-democrats-overwhelmingly-deny-aoc-powerful-committee-seat) [F] (reports Pelosi campaigned for Cuellar).

### Case 6.4: Justice Democrats, 2018 to 2020 (the faction's counter-tool)

- Ocasio-Cortez beat Joe Crowley in June 2018 by 15 points. Roll Call reported Crowley's campaign spent over $3 million and that he did not have help from national Democrats. Bowman beat Eliot Engel in June 2020. Bush beat Lacy Clay in August 2020 (about 49% to 46%).
- **Sources:** [Al Jazeera, Jun 27, 2018](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2018/06/27/democratic-primary-alexandria-ocasio-cortez-ousts-joe-crowley/) [S]; [Common Dreams, Jun 24, 2020](https://commondreams.org/news/2020/06/24/bowman-wins-mondaire-wins-aoc-absolutely-crushes-it-progressive-wave-sweeps-ny) [S]; [Fox News, Aug 2020](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/lacy-clay-vs-cori-bush-missouri-01-dem-primary-hfr-hfr) [S].

### Case 6.5: Schumer and the Maine Senate primary, 2026

- **Who, when:** CBS (June 10, 2026) [F] reports Schumer recruited Gov. Janet Mills, and she dropped out in April 2026 after trailing Platner in polls and fundraising. Platner won the June 9 primary. Schumer, asked repeatedly, said "we're going to beat Susan Collins and take back the Senate."
- **Source:** [CBS News, Jun 10, 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/maine-senate-results-platner-collins/) [F].

### Outcome (tactic 6)

Real money, mixed results for the leaders. The blacklist lasted two years, drew a written Progressive Caucus objection and a boycott campaign, and ended on a new chair's order (case 6.1). UDP spending coincided with two incumbents losing in 2024 (cases 6.2). Evidence for the transfer of power runs to AIPAC-aligned groups and the challengers' side, not to the leaders. The leaders' own primary tool, recruitment (case 6.5), failed in Maine in 2026. Spending by a leadership PAC in primaries: not found this session with dollar amounts.

## 7. The 2025-26 Democratic case

All items below are news from this session. The only fetched pages are TIME (Nov 10, 2025), Fox (Nov 5, 2026), the New Republic (Nov 5, 2026), Newsweek (undated) and CBS (Jun 10, 2026). The rest are [S].

### Case 7.1: Schumer and the March 2025 funding bill

- **Who, when:** Schumer voted to advance the Republican continuing resolution in mid-March 2025. Jeffries called the House bill "un-American" and helped hold House Democrats in near-unanimous opposition. Asked about new leadership in the Senate, Jeffries said "Next question." Rep. Delia Ramirez and Rep. Glenn Ivey called for Schumer to leave. Schumer said "I'm not stepping down" around March 23, 2025. The Senate passed the bill 62-38.
- **Concession:** None found from Schumer or Jeffries.
- **Sources:** [APR/NPR, Mar 13, 2025](https://www.apr.org/politics-government/2025-03-13/deep-sense-of-outrage-and-betrayal-house-democrats-react-to-schumer-announcement) [S]; [Fox News, Mar 2025](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/top-house-democrat-leaves-schumer-cold-amid-lefts-disarray-shutdown-drama) [S]; [Boston Globe, Mar 23, 2025](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/03/23/nation/chuck-schumer-wont-step-down-senate-democratic-leader/) [S].

### Case 7.2: The fall 2025 shutdown

- **Who, when:** In the fall of 2025 Democrats demanded an extension of the ACA enhanced premium tax credits and rejected a clean continuing resolution. Jeffries said "We will not support a partisan spending agreement that continues to rip away health care from the American people, period, full stop" [S]. On November 9 to 10, 2025 eight Senate Democrats voted with Republicans to reopen the government without the ACA extension. Schumer voted no and said "I cannot in good faith support this CR that fails to address the health care crisis."
- **Backlash:** Reps. Rashida Tlaib and Ro Khanna, and Seth Moulton, called for new leadership. Indivisible said it would not back any Senate primary candidate who did not call for Schumer to step down [S]. Jeffries said Schumer held the caucus together for nearly six weeks and won "some concessions."
- **Concession by leaders:** The leaders adopted the base's central demand (the ACA credit extension) as a shutdown condition. They did not win it. No change to leadership rules or posts found.
- **Sources:** [TIME, Nov 10, 2025](https://time.com/7332829/chuck-schumer-shutdown-reelection/) [F]; [Hill, Nov 2025](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/5599623-schumer-shutdown-progressive-anger/) [S]; [Punchbowl](https://punchbowl.news/?p=120024) [S].

### Case 7.3: Jeffries endorses Mamdani, October 24, 2025
